# Individual Footprint

## 1. My Main Responsibility

My primary responsibility was leading the overall conceptualization of ARBIVERSE, developing its integrated storyline, and coordinating game rules, mechanics, and scoring frameworks across all three phases.

I took primary responsibility for developing Phase 3's financial calculation formulas, Excel financial model, and scoring framework. I also co-developed Phase 1's scoring system and conducted scenario-based testing across all three phases to ensure financial accuracy, logical consistency, and balanced gameplay.

My work focused on connecting financial concepts, player decisions, quantitative models, and performance evaluation into a coherent educational trading simulation.

## 2. My Concrete Outputs

### 2.1. Overall Game Concept and Cross-Phase Design

I developed the overall ARBIVERSE concept, connecting three historical financial events into a progressive trading simulation:

- **Phase 1 – Tulip Mania:** Speculative trading, arbitrage opportunities, market sentiment, and bubble-crash risk.
- **Phase 2 – Thai Baht Crisis:** Currency arbitrage, exchange-rate discrepancies, transaction costs, and market restrictions.
- **Phase 3 – JPY Carry Trade:** Currency borrowing, investment allocation, FX exposure, and debt repayment.

I co-developed game mechanics, trading rules, financial constraints, and player decision-making structures across the three phases. I helped ensure consistency between the intended learning objectives, gameplay mechanisms, financial calculations, and scoring criteria.

### 2.2. Phase 3 – Financial Mechanics and Excel Modeling

I took primary responsibility for developing Phase 3's financial mechanics and quantitative calculation framework.

I built an Excel-based financial model covering:

- **JPY Borrowing:** Borrowing decisions, outstanding principal, and financing costs.
- **FX Transactions:** JPY/USD conversion, exchange-rate movements, and FX gains or losses.
- **Portfolio Investment:** Asset allocation, investment returns, and portfolio valuation.
- **Debt Repayment:** Interest expenses, outstanding liabilities, and repayment obligations.
- **Solvency Assessment:** Ability to meet debt obligations under different market conditions.
- **Final Performance:** Net wealth, investment profitability, and the financial consequences of player decisions.

The Excel model simulated different trading strategies and market outcomes, providing a quantitative reference for translating Phase 3's financial logic into the playable game.

This helped ensure that borrowing, FX movements, investment decisions, and debt repayment were evaluated as interconnected financial activities rather than independent transactions.

### 2.3. Scoring Framework Development

I developed and refined scoring frameworks for Phases 1 and 3, focusing on evaluating both financial outcomes and the quality of player decisions.

**Phase 1 – Tulip Mania**

I contributed to designing a 100-point scoring framework consisting of:

| Component | Points |
|-----------|-------:|
| Financial Performance | 40 |
| Arbitrage & Trading Execution | 30 |
| Decision Quality | 15 |
| Risk Management | 15 |
| **Total** | **100** |

I also refined the P&L scoring benchmark based on gameplay testing. After observing that the maximum achievable return was approximately 40%, I adjusted the full-score threshold to a net return of 35%.

The framework also incorporates mechanisms to discourage passive gameplay and unnecessary trading.

**Phase 3 – JPY Carry Trade**

I took primary responsibility for developing the Phase 3 scoring framework, incorporating:

- Financial performance from FX and portfolio investments.
- Risk management related to debt, liquidity, and investment exposure.
- Decision quality in borrowing, capital allocation, position sizing, and repayment.
- Consistency between trading decisions, financial outcomes, and scoring calculations.

The scoring framework was designed to distinguish effective financial decision-making from profitability driven primarily by favorable market movements.

### 2.4. Scenario-Based Testing and Game Refinement

I conducted scenario-based testing across all three phases to verify game mechanics, financial calculations, and scoring consistency.

For Phase 3, I developed and tested 40 scenarios (S1–S40), examining different combinations of borrowing, FX movements, asset allocations, investment returns, and debt repayment outcomes.

Testing focused on:

- Accuracy of P&L and portfolio valuation.
- Interest calculations and outstanding debt.
- Liquidity constraints and solvency conditions.
- Consistency between financial outcomes and scoring results.
- Edge cases and unintended gameplay advantages.

I also tested Phases 1 and 2 to identify logical inconsistencies, unrealistic scoring thresholds, and potential issues affecting game balance.

The findings were used to propose revisions to game rules, financial calculations, and performance evaluation criteria.
